Epithelial Tissue

Closely packed cells that cover the body and line its organs. Open an entry to see how each type is built and what it does.

Overview

What is epithelial tissue?

Where is it?

It forms the outer covering of the body (the skin) and lines internal organs such as the mouth, lungs, blood vessels and intestine.

What is it like?

Its cells are packed closely together with very little space between them. This stops germs from getting in and reduces water loss.

    Common mix-ups

    Layers or shape?

    Simple means one layer and stratified means many layers. Squamous, cuboidal and columnar describe the cell shape: flat, cube-like and pillar-like.

    Cilia or microvilli?

    Cilia are longer hair-like projections that move and push mucus along. Microvilli are tiny folds that stay still and add surface area for absorption.

    Intestine or airway?

    The intestine lining is non-ciliated columnar epithelium with microvilli. The ciliated type lines parts of the respiratory tract.
